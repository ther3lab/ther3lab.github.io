publications

publications by members of the $R^3$ Lab

2026

  1. arXiv
    Three Centuries of the Laws of Cricket Reveal Core Principles of the Evolution of Regulatory Mechanisms
    Daniel Chia, Hyejin Youn, Jonny Singer, and 5 more authors
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2607.05586, 2026
    Shows that the Laws of Cricket have grown as a power law in system size over 300 years – direct evidence for the Lab’s claim that regulatory systems, biological or social, grow according to shared quantitative laws.
    Bib PDF
  2. arXiv
    Branching under First-Passage Resetting
    Aanjaneya Kumar and James Holehouse
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2605.16693, 2026
    Introduces a framework where replication events (e.g. cell division, viral lysis) are triggered by internal first-passage dynamics rather than an external clock, showing that stochastic timing fluctuations actually enhance population growth relative to a deterministic clock. The Lab is building on this same branching-process math to model rule-making as a response to rule-breaking.
    Bib PDF
  3. arXiv
    The Origins of Transient Bimodality
    Kaan Öcal, Augustinas Sukys, Aanjaneya Kumar, and 1 more author
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2607.16531, 2026
    Identifies a phase transition into transient bimodal behavior driven purely by noise and time-dependent kinetics – core evidence for the Lab’s claim that biology is fundamentally transient rather than steady-state.
    arXiv Bib PDF
  4. bioRxiv
    Microbial Ecosystems Reveal a Universal Signature of Ecological Assembly
    James Holehouse, Geoffrey B West, Christopher P Kempes, and 1 more author
    bioRxiv, 2026
    Extends the Lab’s generative model of functional diversity to microbial ecosystems, showing they occupy a distinct region of the same parameter space that also describes proteomes and cities.
    DOI Bib Website
  5. npj Comp.
    Quantifying broken detailed balance in transcription
    James Holehouse
    npj Complexity, 2026
    Derives exact formulas for the thermodynamic irreversibility (entropy production rate) of a two-state gene, and finds that most real genes avoid kinetic regimes with high energy dissipation despite this not being required by thermodynamics alone. Also shows that cell-to-cell variability can make gene expression look more or less irreversible than a single "representative cell" would suggest.
    arXiv Bib PDF
  6. Proc. B
    Do distinct subpopulations signify modes of behaviour in a noisy single cell?
    James Holehouse
    Proceedings of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences, 2026
    Argues that counting subpopulations in single-cell data can be a misleading proxy for the true number of behavioral modes available to a cell, and constructs counterexamples where two apparent subpopulations actually arise from non-equilibrium transient dynamics rather than distinct stable states.
    Bib PDF
  7. arXiv
    How is gene-regulatory evolution affected by cell-to-cell variability?
    Leonardo Ivan Estrella Dzib and James Holehouse
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2604.26082, 2026
    Submitted
    Extends a classical model of gene-regulatory network evolution so a single network can express a distribution of phenotypes through noisy developmental dynamics, finding that higher noise actually promotes better-aligned, more mutation-buffered networks – a direct link between the Lab’s work on regulatory evolution and its work on robustness to noise.
    PDF
  8. Nat. Sci. Data
    A Dataset Showing a Century of Evolution in the Complexity of the United States Legal Code
    Dawoon Jeong*, James Holehouse*, Jisung Yoon, and 3 more authors
    Scientific Data, 2026
    Provides the century-long U.S. Code dataset that anchors the Lab’s claim that legal and biological regulatory systems both grow as power laws in system size.
    arXiv Bib PDF
  9. PNAS
    Scaling laws for function diversity and specialization across socioeconomic and biological complex systems
    Vicky Chuqiao Yang*, James Holehouse*, Hyejin Youn, and 4 more authors
    Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 2026
    Establishes the Lab’s central scaling-law result: that function diversity and specialization scale similarly across socioeconomic and biological complex systems.
    arXiv Bib PDF

2024

  1. JCP
    Solving the time-dependent protein distributions for autoregulated bursty gene expression using spectral decomposition
    Bingjie Wu, James Holehouse, Ramon Grima, and 1 more author
    The Journal of Chemical Physics, 2024
    Derives an exact time-dependent solution for autoregulated bursty gene expression, showing the eigenfunctions are Heun functions and the eigenvalues follow from a continued-fraction equation – a genuinely transient, non-steady-state result in the same vein as the Lab’s later work on regulatory transience.
    Bib PDF Website
  2. Phys. Rev. E
    First passage on disordered intervals
    James Holehouse and S Redner
    Physical Review E, 2024
    Derives exact first-passage statistics for a random walk on a finite interval with disordered hopping rates, finding that different realizations of the disorder produce wildly different first-passage times, including genuinely bimodal first-passage-time distributions.
    Bib PDF Website
  3. ESMTB Comms.
    Model reduction, mechanistic modelling and transience in models of stochastic chemical kinetics
    James Holehouse
    European Communications in Mathematical and Theoretical Biology, 2024
    Summarizes the PhD thesis work that seeded the Lab’s research program on transience and model reduction in stochastic gene expression.
    Bib PDF Website

2023

  1. Sci. Advances
    The minimal intrinsic stochasticity of constitutively expressed eukaryotic genes is sub-Poissonian
    Douglas E Weidemann, James Holehouse, Abhyudai Singh, and 2 more authors
    Science Advances, 2023
    Identifies fission-yeast genes whose expression noise falls genuinely below the previously assumed Poissonian floor, and shows this "sub-Poissonian" regime arises from a higher mRNA export rate suppressing cytoplasmic noise.
    Bib PDF Website
  2. Entropy
    Recurrence and Eigenfunction Methods for Non-Trivial Models of Discrete Binary Choice
    James Holehouse
    Entropy, 2023
    Derives a closed-form solution to the three-term recurrence relation that governs relaxation dynamics in binary-choice models like Kirman’s ant-recruitment model, using the power-series structure of Heun functions.
    Bib PDF Website

2022

  1. J. Phys. Complexity
    Exact time-dependent dynamics of discrete binary choice models
    James Holehouse and José Moran
    Journal of Physics: Complexity, 2022
    Derives exact time-dependent solutions for discrete binary-choice models with agent interactions – including Kirman and Föllmer’s ant-recruitment model for any finite population – showing convergence to equilibrium depends only on the random switching rate, not on imitation rate or population size.
    Bib PDF Website
  2. PLoS One
    Non-equilibrium time-dependent solution to discrete choice with social interactions
    James Holehouse and Hector Pollitt
    PLoS ONE, 2022
    Solves Brock and Durlauf’s binary-decision model away from equilibrium, revealing metastable effects and path-dependent behavior for highly rational agents, and extends the model to altruistic as well as selfish decision-makers.
    Bib PDF Website

2021

  1. J. R. Soc. Interface
    Distinguishing between models of mammalian gene expression: telegraph-like models versus mechanistic models
    Svitlana Braichenko*, James Holehouse*, and Ramon Grima
    Journal of the Royal Society Interface, 2021
    Compares simplified telegraph-like models against a detailed mechanistic model of mammalian transcription, finding the two are practically indistinguishable from mRNA count distributions alone but can be told apart from their waiting-time distributions.
    Bib PDF Website

    James Holehouse, Zhixing Cao, and Ramon Grima
    Biophysical journal, 2020
    A review comparing the major stochastic models of autoregulatory genetic feedback loops used in the literature, summarizing what is and isn’t known about how they relate to one another.
    Bib PDF Website
  2. J. Phys. A
    Steady-state fluctuations of a genetic feedback loop with fluctuating rate parameters using the unified colored noise approximation
    James Holehouse, Abhishek Gupta, and Ramon Grima
    Journal of Physics A: Mathematical and Theoretical, 2020
    Uses the unified colored noise approximation to show how fluctuations in a gene circuit’s own rate parameters reshape its protein distribution, and identifies when this approximation breaks down.
    Bib PDF Website
  3. JCP
    Stochastic time-dependent enzyme kinetics: closed-form solution and transient bimodality
    James Holehouse, Augustinas Sukys, and Ramon Grima
    The Journal of Chemical Physics, 2020
    Derives a closed-form solution for stochastic Michaelis-Menten enzyme kinetics and shows the substrate distribution can become transiently bimodal at intermediate times – a behavior with no deterministic counterpart.
    Bib PDF Website

2019

  1. Biophysical Journal
    Revisiting the reduction of stochastic models of genetic feedback loops with fast promoter switching
    James Holehouse and Ramon Grima
    Biophysical journal, 2019
    Shows that the standard fast-promoter-switching approximation for stochastic gene feedback loops breaks down whenever the protein-DNA binding rate greatly exceeds the unbinding rate, meaning commonly used reduced models are only accurate in part of the relevant parameter space.
    Bib PDF Website
