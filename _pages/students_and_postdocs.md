For those interested in joining the lab, the recommended reading list is:

- Exploring complexity by Nicolis and Prigogine (see [here](https://www.amazon.com/Exploring-Complexity-Introduction-Gregoire-Nicolis/dp/0716718596)). Classic introduction to a broad array of complexity science and mathematical biology.
- Stochastic Processes in Physics and Chemistry by van Kampen (see [here](https://www.amazon.com/Stochastic-Processes-Chemistry-North-Holland-Personal/dp/0444529659)). The go-to book for learning stochastic methods for biochemical kinetics.
- Approximation and inference methods for stochastic biochemical kinetics—a tutorial review (see [here](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1751-8121/aa54d9/meta)). Great review on stochastic methods for gene regulatory mechanisms.
- Diversity and Complexity (see [here](https://www.amazon.com/Diversity-Complexity-Primers-Complex-Systems/dp/0691137676)). Fun read on the role of diversity across biological and social systems.
- Robustness and Evolvability in Living Systems (see [here](https://www.amazon.com/Robustness-Evolvability-Systems-Princeton-Complexity/dp/0691134049)). One of my favorite books in science—emphasizes the importance of robustness across multiple scales of biology.
- The Complex World: An Introduction to the Foundations of Complexity Science (see [here](https://www.amazon.com/Complex-World-Introduction-Foundations-Complexity/dp/1947864629)). Update to date summary of the philosophy of complex systems science.
- Synergetics: Introduction and Advanced Topics (see here). Another favorite of mine—summary of many of the methods used in the R3 lab accompanied by a deep philosophy of self-organizing systems.
